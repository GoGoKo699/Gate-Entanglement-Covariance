# Current Letter concept

Working title: **Boundary gates and equilibrium entanglement memory**.

A local gate applied to a global Haar state leaves the entropy distribution
unchanged. What it changes is the correlation between the old and new entropy.
We derive this correlation for every fixed positive Rényi order from the gate's
operator Schmidt spectrum, for fixed boundary support and growing balanced
spectators.

The central theorem is the mode-resolved covariance law. Three short consequences
make its content concrete: higher operator moments distinguish gates with equal
operator purity; fixed access imposes a memory floor, sharp for equal active boundary dimensions; and every dual-unitary
gate reaches that floor even though their product-input entangling powers differ.
Boundary SWAP and phaseSWAP illustrate the last consequence. Their exact finite-d
purity formulas show how the common limit emerges and why whole-half SWAP cannot
be substituted into it.

The evidence should remain compact: the analytical derivation, one exact finite-size
control, and the saved two-size non-diagonal illustration. The small-alpha temporal
exponent is a secondary consequence, with the Gaussian-interpolation antecedent
stated explicitly.

The mathematical fluctuation framework and operator-entanglement/OTOC connections
are prior work and must be credited. The candidate contribution is their explicit
reduction for every fixed positive Rényi order for a physical boundary operation and the resulting limits
on what equilibrium memory distinguishes. This document is a concept, not a
submission-ready manuscript or a claim of established PRL significance.
