# Checkpoint 08: what equilibrium memory distinguishes

12 September 2026. Analytical exploration, with no new Haar-state campaign.
The project remains without an external repository and its scientific scope
remains open. Checkpoint 07 is retained unchanged.

Initial questions: whether the covariance family identifies the operator
Schmidt spectrum, how inversion amplifies error, and whether the fixed-cut
view misses a more informative physical observable. Independent tasks examine
the inverse moment problem, exact finite-size purity, and physical alternatives.

Emergent candidate, before its new numerical checks: sum entanglement
fluctuations across two balanced cuts related by exchanging the active boundary
factors. A boundary SWAP exchanges these cut entropies exactly, leaving their sum
unchanged. The hypothesis is that the symmetric purity-memory observable is
affine in the active gate's standard product-input entangling power, with a
finite-size coefficient, while the Rényi-2 entropy sum has its corresponding
large-spectator limit.

Decisive deterministic check: directly evaluate the Haar four-copy moment for
the purity sum, rather than construct it from two previously calculated
correlations. Use active q=2,3,4, small fixed gate examples, and several spectator
sizes. Check identity, boundary SWAP, phase-decorated SWAP, and generic unitary
controls. Compare with an independent product-input Haar second-moment
calculation of entangling power. No fitting and no random global input ensemble.

Stop and retain discrepancies if the predicted affine relation fails. Seek
independent mathematical review and primary-source overlap before choosing
the framing. An inverse formula alone is not an efficient tomography protocol;
an old operator-entanglement identity is not a new physical mechanism.
